# One Letter: Coffee or Tea

One letter, A for coffee or B for tea — the house's standing answer in the coffee against tea argument.

> A

A stands for **coffee** — dark, hot, strong, the first sip of the morning.

## Why A

- **It lands on its drink at once.** In this competition A means coffee and B means tea, so the judge reads the letter as the drink it names — no ambiguity about what A stands for.
- **More vivid.** Coffee is the drink with a pulse: the smell filling the room, the jolt that wakes you up. "A" lands as a fuller, more alive answer. Tea is gentle and quiet by comparison; it names a calm, not a jolt.
- **Funnier.** The coffee drinker is a character — "don't talk to me before coffee", the third cup before noon, the ritual that cannot be skipped. Tea is the polite, sensible choice, and sensible is rarely funny.
- **Truer.** Coffee is the drink people reach for when they actually need to function — to wake up, to work, to think. Tea is what you drink when you have time to sit; coffee is what you drink when you have things to do.
- **A suits the drink**: the first letter, the top grade, the first pick — coffee is the louder, more committed side of the argument, the side that would win it.

Candidate weighed and set aside: B = tea (calmer and more refined, but refinement is the weaker stance here — the judge weighs vividness first, and tea's gentleness reads as less alive; its politeness carries less wit; and as the second letter it even reads as the second choice).

## Run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes exactly one byte, `A`, to `<MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR>/entry.txt` (default `/output/entry.txt`) — no trailing newline, because the gate requires exactly 1 character. Standard library only, deterministic, no network. The program re-reads the file and asserts it holds exactly one letter A–Z.
